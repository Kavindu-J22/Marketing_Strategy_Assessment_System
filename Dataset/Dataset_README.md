# Synthetic dataset for Marketing Strategy Assessment (Sri Lanka)

**DATA PROVENANCE: 100% SYNTHETIC / SIMULATED.** The 5,000 rows do not describe 5,000 actual people, actual technology companies, field-survey responses, expert judgments, or observed marketing campaigns. They are fictional **company-and-campaign scenarios** created for software development, data-pipeline tests, preliminary model experiments, interface demos, and sensitivity testing. Do **not** claim this as original survey data in a thesis or use simulated labels and metrics as evidence of real-world marketing effectiveness.

## Provided files

- `Marketing_Questionnaire_Synthetic_5000.csv`: 5,000 scenarios, the original 21 questionnaire questions represented as Q1–Q21 fields, and additional fields documenting informed refinements.
- `Audience_Classifier_ML_5000.csv`: 5,000 ML feature cases with simulated reference class (`Low`, `Medium`, `High`), a rule baseline, and explicit training exclusions.
- `Marketing_Channel_Budget_5000.csv`: 5,000 illustrative channel recommendations, scores, campaign lengths, and monthly and campaign budget amounts. These are recommendations from **heuristics**, not measured results.
- `Dataset_Data_Dictionary.csv`: schema, purpose and recommended model use for each column.
- `Dataset_Generation_Audit.json`: reproducibility seed, class and industry distributions, budgets, split counts and integrity checks.
- `build_marketing_synthetic_dataset.py`: reproducible generator (Python standard library).

## Generation method

- 1,000 fictional technology-company profiles across 8 survey industry categories.
- Five campaign scenarios per profile to obtain 5,000 rows. Thus, 5,000 **records** do **not** mean 5,000 independent company respondents.
- Product features and descriptions generated from industry-specific fictional software/service patterns. No real people, authentic company identifiers or contacts included.
- Target customer segments, regions, objectives, monthly budgets, campaign durations and channel histories generated by controlled simulation with seeded pseudo-random variation.
- Questionnaire Q14 fixes the benefits-versus-pain-points confusion by storing **customer problems**, not the benefits presented in the original choices.
- Q16 uses `No performance data` or a subset of the channels in Q15 (instead of selecting a never-used channel).
- Q10 and Q11 each use their five-point question scale. Their numeric versions are additional input variables. Simulated literacy labels are assigned from a *hidden synthetic aptitude*, with noisy observations; these are **not validated customer proficiency scores**.
- Q12 is a communication-detail *preference*, not proof of proficiency. Do not use it as a classifier feature when aiming to generalize from literacy to preferred messaging; the current classifier-ready file intentionally omits it.
- Channel ranking uses an illustrative AHP-style weighted multi-criteria score: audience fit **34%**, campaign-objective fit **25%**, cost suitability **18%**, reach fit **13%**, content match **10%**, with synthetic variation. **Weights have not been elicited from experts**; consistency of a mathematically constructed ratio matrix is not expert validation.
- Rank top 2 channels for monthly budget < LKR 15,000; otherwise top 3 channels. Allocations are rounded in LKR 1,000 units and the final amount absorbs the residue. Monthly allocations sum exactly to the stated monthly budget. Zero-budget cases allocate LKR 0.
- KPI numbers are merely **synthetic campaign targets**, not traffic/leads/sales that occurred.

## Group-aware train / validation / test

- Train: 750 profiles × 5 = **3,750 records**.
- Validation: 150 profiles × 5 = **750 records**.
- Test: 100 profiles × 5 = **500 records**.
- Each `company_profile_id` occurs in exactly **one** split. Split by company, **not** randomly by scenario row, so correlated campaign variants do not leak into the test set.
- For audience prediction, target is `technical_literacy_reference_label_SIMULATED`. Candidate predictors in the ML CSV are `industry`, `market_type`, `customer_role`, `age_group`, `location`, `customer_audience_text`, `technology_comfort_1to5`, and `technical_terms_familiarity_1to5`.
- **Exclude** the target itself, `rule_baseline_label`, `scenario_id`, `company_profile_id`, `ml_split`, `label_origin`, the `forbidden_training_fields_note`, and any generated score/target fields from the predictor matrix. Do not use simulated class rates or agreement with their generating rules as research validity claims.

## Data collection and evidence required for a real dissertation

1. Administer the revised questionnaire to consented real technology-company stakeholders, document sample size, selection, missingness and possible sampling bias.
2. Independently obtain customer-technical-literacy assessments or qualified human labels on held-out target-audience descriptions. Company representatives' estimates alone are proxy measurements.
3. Elicit AHP pairwise comparisons from marketing experts, aggregate judgments, publish criteria and consistency ratios. Compare rankings with expert and rule-based baselines.
4. Assess factual faithfulness, clarity, audience fit and usefulness of AI-generated marketing text using blinded reviewers; this dataset's generated messages are **templates**, not expert-rated AI outputs.
5. Validate budget recommendations on actual campaigns or appropriate real benchmark scenarios, without claiming that hypothetical allocations increase ROI.
6. Report performance separately on **real unseen** cases and explain whether simulations were used for pretraining, testing, augmentation or demos.

## Reproducibility / limitations

Generation date: **2026-10-20**. Seed: **20261020**. Python standard library, no outside dataset download. All synthetic rates and scenario correlations are **design assumptions**; matching any real-world statistic is coincidental unless explicitly stated and supported. Public digital-reach figures are broad background and were **not used** to reverse-engineer the row distributions. A simulated baseline classification match rate is not an empirical accuracy measurement.
